 1377: The world’s first quarantine begins

 On this day in 1377, lawmakers in Ragusa—the Adriatic merchant republic we now call [Dubrovnik](https://www.britannica.com/place/Dubrovnik)— instituted the first [quarantine](https://www.britannica.com/science/quarantine-preventive-medicine) in the world so as to manage the spread of the [Black Death](https://www.britannica.com/event/Black-Death). Anyone arriving from a plague-stricken place, whether on foot or by sea, would be made to wait 30 days (later extended to 40) in isolation before entering the city. The quarantine was a success—Ragusa passed a full 44 years without any instances of the plague and shook off outbreaks that decimated the population of other regions.

Terrorist attack at Atlanta Olympics

 On this day in 1996, a pipe bomb exploded in Olympic Centennial Park in [Atlanta](https://www.britannica.com/event/Atlanta-1996-Olympic-Games), Georgia, killing 1 person and injuring 111 in the first [terrorist attack](https://www.britannica.com/topic/terrorism) at the [Olympics](https://www.britannica.com/sports/Olympic-Games) since the [1972 Games in Munich](https://www.britannica.com/event/Munich-1972-Olympic-Games), West Germany. In 2025 domestic terrorist [Eric Rudolph](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Eric-Rudolph) pleaded guilty to this attack and three others. He was sentenced to four consecutive life sentences.
