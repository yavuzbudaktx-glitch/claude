 1775: The USPS’s revolutionary origins

 As the [American Revolution](https://www.britannica.com/event/American-Revolution) loomed, the rebelling colonists had a problem. Reliable communication was vital to the war effort, but under British rule, mail was not private.In 1774 William Goddard,[Benjamin Franklin](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Benjamin-Franklin)’s former publishing partner, came up with a solution: He created an independent mail system with trusted riders who transported mail under lock and key. On this day in 1775, the [Second Continental Congress](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Continental-Congress) adopted Goddard’s system, creating what became the [U.S. Postal Service](https://www.britannica.com/topic/United-States-Postal-Service), and appointed Franklin postmaster general.
