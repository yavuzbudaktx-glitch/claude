[Introducing the first typewriter](https://www.britannica.com/today-in-history/June-23-1868-Patent-for-First-Typewriter)

 On this day in 1868 American inventors [Christopher Latham Sholes](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Christopher-Latham-Sholes), Samuel W. Soulé, and Carlos Glidden were granted a [patent](https://www.britannica.com/topic/patent) for the first [typewriter](https://www.britannica.com/technology/typewriter). The concept was revolutionary, and within a few years the typewriter—and its efficient QWERTY keyboard—had become an indispensable office tool and a fixture in the literary world. _[[Read today’s edition of Today in History >>](https://www.britannica.com/today-in-history/June-23-1868-Patent-for-First-Typewriter)]_

Clarence Thomas

Born this day in 1948 was [Clarence Thomas](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Clarence-Thomas), associate justice of the [U.S. Supreme Court](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Supreme-Court-of-the-United-States) from 1991, whose appointment to replace [Thurgood Marshall](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Thurgood-Marshall) (the court’s first African American justice) gave the court a conservative cast.

2016

 The [United Kingdom](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Kingdom) voted in a referendum to withdraw from the [European Union](https://www.britannica.com/topic/European-Union), with 51.9 percent supporting Britain’s exit (“[Brexit](https://www.britannica.com/)”) and 48.1 percent opposing the move; it marked the first time a country had decided to leave the organization. _[[Test your knowledge of British culture and politics](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/british-culture-and-politics).]_
