 1850: A Florida doctor invents the ice machine

 On this day in 1850 machine-made ice debuted at a Florida party, astonishing guests accustomed to imported natural ice. Its inventor, physician John Gorrie, had originally created the machine to cool [yellow fever](https://www.britannica.com/science/yellow-fever) patients. Although he was mocked during his lifetime, his breakthrough became a crucial step toward modern [refrigeration](https://www.britannica.com/technology/refrigeration) and [air conditioning](https://www.britannica.com/technology/air-conditioning).

![Image 7: Storming of the Bastille](https://cdn.britannica.com/83/191383-050-04962456/Storming-of-the-Bastille-July-14-1789.jpg?w=356&h=200&c=crop)

[Storming of the Bastille](https://www.britannica.com/event/storming-of-the-Bastille)

 On this day in 1789 a mob advanced on the [Bastille](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Bastille) in [Paris](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris), demanding the arms and munitions stored there. When the guards resisted, the crowd captured the prison, an act that symbolized the [end of the ancien régime](https://www.britannica.com/event/French-Revolution). _[[How much do you know about French history?](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/france-a-history-quiz)]_
