 1958: Brazil’s first World Cup title

 On this day in 1958, [Brazil](https://www.britannica.com/place/Brazil) defeated [Sweden](https://www.britannica.com/place/Sweden), 5–2, to win the country’s first [World Cup](https://www.britannica.com/sports/World-Cup-football). The star was 17-year-old [Pelé](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Pele-Brazilian-football-player), who scored twice during the match, becoming the youngest player to score in a World Cup final. Brazil defended its title in 1962 and went on to win three more World Cups (1970, 1994, and 2002), making it the most successful country in the history of the tournament.

[London’s Globe Theatre destroyed by fire](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Globe-Theatre)

 During a performance of [William Shakespeare](https://www.britannica.com/biography/William-Shakespeare)’s [Henry VIII](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Henry-VIII-by-Shakespeare) on this day in 1613, the [Globe Theatre](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Globe-Theatre) was destroyed within an hour after its thatch was accidentally set aflame by a cannon marking the king’s entrance onstage. _[[Take our history of theatre quiz](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/a-history-of-theatre-quiz).]_

1767

 Parliament passed the [Townshend Revenue Act](https://www.britannica.com/event/Townshend-Acts), sponsored by British Chancellor of the Exchequer [Charles Townshend](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Charles-Townshend-British-statesman), which imposed on colonists in British America an import tax on tea and other goods and thus brought some colonists one step closer to revolution. _[[Test your knowledge of early American history](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/early-america).]_
