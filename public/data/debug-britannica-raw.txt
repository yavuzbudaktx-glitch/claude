 1897: Launch of Salomon August Andrée’s balloon-based polar expedition

 On this day Swedish engineer Salomon August Andrée launched an ambitious plan to become the first explorer to see the [North Pole](https://www.britannica.com/place/North-Pole). Instead of attempting this by foot, Andrée, engineer Knut Frænkel, and photographer Nils Strindberg would travel via hydrogen-filled [balloon](https://www.britannica.com/technology/balloon)—a “ship” they named Örnen (Swedish for “eagle”). Within an hour of their launch from the [Svalbard](https://www.britannica.com/place/Svalbard) archipelago, about 600 miles from their target, the balloon had drifted so high that spectators lost sight of it. The men were never seen alive again.

Duel between Aaron Burr and Alexander Hamilton

 Outraged over disparaging remarks that [Alexander Hamilton](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Alexander-Hamilton-United-States-statesman) had allegedly made at a dinner party, [Aaron Burr](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Aaron-Burr) challenged his longtime rival to a [duel](https://www.britannica.com/event/Burr-Hamilton-duel), and on this day in 1804 he fatally shot Hamilton in [Weehawken](https://www.britannica.com/place/Weehawken), New Jersey. _[[Read more about this and other famous duels](https://www.britannica.com/list/8-famous-duels-and-1-almost-duel).]_

Robert the Bruce

Born this day in 1274, Scottish king [Robert the Bruce](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Robert-the-Bruce) freed [Scotland](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland) from [English rule](https://www.britannica.com/place/England), winning the decisive [Battle of Bannockburn](https://www.britannica.com/event/Battle-of-Bannockburn) (1314) and ultimately confirming Scottish independence in the Treaty of Northampton (1328).
