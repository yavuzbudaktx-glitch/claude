![Image 6: June 22, 1986: Divine assist or Devil's trick?](https://cdn.britannica.com/97/289297-050-390B1113/today-in-history-Maradonas-Hand-of-God.jpg?w=356&h=200&c=crop)

[Maradona’s infamous “Hand of God” play](https://www.britannica.com/today-in-history/June-22-The-Infamous-Hand-of-God-Play)

 On this day in 1986 Argentine [football (soccer)](https://www.britannica.com/sports/football-soccer) player [Diego Maradona](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Diego-Maradona) scored his memorable “[Hand of God](https://www.britannica.com/sports/Famous-FIFA-World-Cup-Goals-Hand-of-God)” goal (the ball struck his hand, but the referee mistakenly thought it had hit his head) in a 1986 [FIFA World Cup](https://www.britannica.com/sports/World-Cup-football) quarterfinal game against [England](https://www.britannica.com/place/England). The illegal goal helped [Argentina](https://www.britannica.com/place/Argentina) win the match, and they went on to win the tournament—their second FIFA World Cup title. _[[Read today’s edition of Today in History >>](https://www.britannica.com/today-in-history/June-22-The-Infamous-Hand-of-God-Play)]_

## More on June 22

![Image 8: George Carlin](https://cdn.britannica.com/23/149023-050-67AE9239/George-Carlin.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 Carlin Productions

2008

 American comedian [George Carlin](https://www.britannica.com/biography/George-Carlin)—whose “Seven Words You Can Never Say on Television” routine led to a [U.S. Supreme Court](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Supreme-Court-of-the-United-States) ruling that gave the [Federal Communications Commission](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Federal-Communications-Commission) (FCC) the right to determine when to censor radio and TV broadcasts—died at age 71. _[[Sort fact from fiction in our quiz about pop culture](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/pop-quiz-fact-or-fiction).]_

![Image 9: Charon](https://cdn.britannica.com/33/183533-050-2957012E/moon-Pluto-photo-Charon-New-Horizons-spacecraft-July-13-2015.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 NASA/Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory/Southwest Research Institute

1978

[Charon](https://www.britannica.com/place/Charon-astronomy), the largest moon of [Pluto](https://www.britannica.com/place/Pluto-dwarf-planet), was discovered. _[[How much do you know about Pluto?](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/pluto)]_

![Image 10: Cuyahoga River: freighter](https://cdn.britannica.com/12/177012-050-87917F5D/bulk-freighter-material-bank-Cleveland-Ohio-Cuyahoga.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 © StonePhotos/Shutterstock.com

1969

 In [Cleveland](https://www.britannica.com/place/Cleveland-Ohio) the severely polluted [Cuyahoga River](https://www.britannica.com/place/Cuyahoga-River) caught on fire when an oil slick floating on the surface ignited; although it was not the first fire on the river, the incident garnered national attention and led to antipollution measures that substantially improved the river’s condition. _[[Read Britannica’s Timeline of Environmental History](https://www.britannica.com/story/a-timeline-of-environmental-history).]_

![Image 11: Lady and the Tramp](https://cdn.britannica.com/29/175329-050-10B5D3CD/scene-Lady-and-the-Tramp.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 © The Walt Disney Company

1955

 The animated musical [Lady and the Tramp](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Lady-and-the-Tramp), one of [Walt Disney](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Walt-Disney)’s most endearing movies, was released in U.S. theaters. _[[Test your knowledge of Walt Disney](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/disney-quiz).]_

![Image 12: Operation Barbarossa](https://cdn.britannica.com/01/150101-050-810CE9A9/soldiers-German-part-Soviet-Union-Operation-Barbarossa-1941.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 NARA/U.S. Department of Defense

1941

 Germany violated the [German-Soviet Nonaggression Pact](https://www.britannica.com/event/German-Soviet-Nonaggression-Pact) of 1939 and attacked the [Soviet Union](https://www.britannica.com/place/Soviet-Union) during [World War II](https://www.britannica.com/event/World-War-II). _[[Sort fact from fiction in our World War II quiz](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/world-war-ii-fact-or-fiction).]_

![Image 13: Philippe Pétain](https://cdn.britannica.com/58/9658-050-3B61D43D/Philippe-Petain.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 Encyclopædia Britannica, Inc.

1940

 With an invading German force at its door in mid-June 1940, the [French](https://www.britannica.com/place/France) government under the leadership of Marshal [Philippe Pétain](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Philippe-Petain) signed an armistice with [Germany](https://www.britannica.com/place/Germany), thereby creating [Vichy France](https://www.britannica.com/event/Vichy-France) (the “French State”). _[[Test your knowledge of French history](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/exploring-french-history).]_

![Image 14: Katherine Dunham](https://cdn.britannica.com/56/8256-050-84507C4F/Katherine-Dunham-Tropical-Revue-1943.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 Courtesy of the Dance Collection, the New York Public Library at Lincoln Center, Astor, Lenox and Tilden Foundations

1910

[Katherine Dunham](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Katherine-Dunham), an African American dancer, choreographer, and [anthropologist](https://www.britannica.com/science/anthropology) noted for her innovative interpretations of primitive, ritualistic, and ethnic [dances](https://www.britannica.com/art/dance/Types-of-dance#ref25700), was born. _[[Take our dance quiz](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/all-about-dance-quiz).]_

![Image 15: Billy Wilder](https://cdn.britannica.com/03/222603-050-6032A0B4/Billy-Wilder-Sunset-Boulevard.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 © 1950 Paramount Pictures

1906

 American motion-picture director and producer [Billy Wilder](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Billy-Wilder), who was known for films that humorously treat subjects of controversy and offer biting indictments of hypocrisy in American life, was born in Sucha, Austria (now in Poland). _[[Test your knowledge of film](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/film-buff).]_

![Image 16: Julian Huxley](https://cdn.britannica.com/55/10855-050-C5587EC8/Julian-Huxley-skull-elephant-1967.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 Horst Tappe/Encyclopædia Britannica, Inc.

1887

 English biologist and philosopher [Julian Huxley](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Julian-Huxley), who greatly influenced the modern development of [embryology](https://www.britannica.com/science/embryology), was born in London. _[[How much do you know about science?](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/science-quiz)]_

![Image 17: Jacques-Louis David: Napoleon in His Study](https://cdn.britannica.com/54/65154-050-0891EC38/Napoleon-in-His-Study-Jacques-Louis-David-Washington-1812.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 Courtesy National Gallery of Art, Washington, D.C. Samuel H. Kress Collection, 1961.9.15

1815

[Napoleon](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Napoleon-I) abdicated as French emperor for the second time. _[[Take our Napoleon Bonaparte quiz](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/all-about-napoleon-bonaparte).]_

![Image 18: Galileo](https://cdn.britannica.com/29/18829-050-3F57E4F8/Galileo-Justus-Sustermans-Uffizi-Gallery-Florence-1637.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 SCALA/Art Resource, New York

1633

 Accused of heresy by the [Inquisition](https://www.britannica.com/topic/inquisition), [Galileo](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Galileo-Galilei) was forced to recant his support of the [Copernican system](https://www.britannica.com/science/Copernican-system), which held that the [Sun](https://www.britannica.com/place/Sun) was the center of the solar system. _[[Test your knowledge of astronomy and space](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/astronomy-and-space-quiz).]_

![Image 19: Henry Hudson](https://cdn.britannica.com/09/62109-050-B0676AEE/Henry-Hudson.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 © North Wind Picture Archives

1611

 English explorer [Henry Hudson](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Henry-Hudson)—who earlier had tried to discover a short route from [Europe](https://www.britannica.com/place/Europe) to [Asia](https://www.britannica.com/place/Asia) through the [Arctic Ocean](https://www.britannica.com/place/Arctic-Ocean)—was set adrift with his son and seven others in [Hudson Bay](https://www.britannica.com/place/Hudson-Bay) by mutineers. _[[Take our quiz about exploration and discovery](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/exploration-and-discovery).]_

![Image 20: default image](https://cdn.britannica.com/mendel-resources/3-188/images/shared/thistle-blue-half.svg?v=3.188.11)

 Image: Encyclopædia Britannica, Inc.

168 bce

 The Romans defeated the [Macedonians](https://www.britannica.com/place/Macedonia-ancient-kingdom-Europe) under King [Perseus](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Perseus-king-of-Macedonia) at the [Battle of Pydna](https://www.britannica.com/event/Battle-of-Pydna), which marked the end of the Macedonian monarchy and allowed [Rome](https://www.britannica.com/place/ancient-Rome)’s annexation of Macedonia.
