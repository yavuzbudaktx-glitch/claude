 2015: NASA discovers an earthlike planet

 On this day in 2015,[NASA](https://www.britannica.com/topic/NASA)announced the discovery of[Earth](https://www.britannica.com/place/Earth)’s “bigger, older cousin,” an [exoplanet](https://www.britannica.com/science/extrasolar-planet) named Kepler-452b. Scientists are excited because it shares similarities with Earth, raising the possibility that it could potentially sustain life. However, with our current technology, the planet is too far away to detect the chemical signs of life. It is so distant that its light takes 1,800 years to reach Earth.

 On this day in 1952, the Free Officers, a nationalistic military group organized by Colonel [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Gamal-Abdel-Nasser), led a coup that overthrew King [Farouk I](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Farouk-I) of [Egypt](https://www.britannica.com/place/Egypt), ending the monarchy and making Major General [Muḥammad Naguib](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Muhammad-Naguib) the country’s notional leader. _[[How much do you know about Egyptian history?](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/egyptian-history-quiz)]_

1967

 Residents of Detroit’s predominately Black neighborhoods began clashing with members of the city’s police department after a police raid on an illegal drinking club. The [violent confrontations](https://www.britannica.com/event/Detroit-Riot-of-1967), which lasted for five days and killed 43 people, helped to catalyze the [Black Power movement](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Black-Power-movement). _[[Learn more about notable riots in U.S. history](https://www.britannica.com/list/theres-a-riot-goin-on-riots-in-us-history-part-two).]_

 Encyclopædia Britannica, Inc.

1962

 The International Agreement on the Neutrality of [Laos](https://www.britannica.com/place/Laos) was signed in [Geneva](https://www.britannica.com/place/Geneva-Switzerland) by 14 countries, including the [USSR](https://www.britannica.com/place/Soviet-Union), North and South [Vietnam](https://www.britannica.com/place/Vietnam), and the [United States](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-States). The signatories committed to respect Laos’s territorial integrity and neutrality, in part by not interfering in its internal affairs nor introducing military personnel on its territory.
