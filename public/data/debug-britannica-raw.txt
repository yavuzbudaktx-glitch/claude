 1859: Daredevil crosses Niagara on a tightrope

 On this day in 1859, Jean-François Gravelet crossed from the U.S. to Canada on a 1,100-foot tightrope, suspended about 170 feet above the raging waters of [Niagara Falls](https://www.britannica.com/place/Niagara-Falls-waterfall-North-America).Along the way, the French acrobat, better known by the stage name “Blondin”(a reference to his bright blond hair), performed various tricks (lying down, drinking wine) to thousands of cheering spectators. He repeated the feat an estimated 300 times, with new gimmicks added for each crossing—at one point he even cooked an omelet.

Night of the Long Knives

 On this date in 1934 occurred the “[Night of the Long Knives](https://www.britannica.com/event/Night-of-the-Long-Knives),” in which [Adolf Hitler](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Adolf-Hitler) had his [SS](https://www.britannica.com/topic/SS) guards summarily execute many leading officials of the [SA](https://www.britannica.com/topic/SA-Nazi-organization), a [Nazi](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Nazi-Party) paramilitary group, and hundreds of other perceived enemies.

## More on June 30

![Image 9: Donald Trump](https://cdn.britannica.com/68/216668-050-DD3A9D0A/United-States-President-Donald-Trump-2017.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 White House photograph by Shealah Craighead/Library of Congress, Washington, D.C. (reproduction no. LC-DIG-ppbd-00608)

2019

 While at the [DMZ](https://www.britannica.com/place/demilitarized-zone-Korean-peninsula), President [Donald Trump](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump) walked into [North Korea](https://www.britannica.com/place/North-Korea) to greet its leader, [Kim Jong-Un](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kim-Jong-Eun), thus becoming the first sitting U.S. president to visit that country. _[[Test your knowledge of presidential firsts](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/us-presidential-firsts).]_

![Image 10: Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge in China](https://cdn.britannica.com/75/274175-050-5B37FF36/Danyang-Kunshan-grand-bridge-the-worlds-longest-bridge-Shanghai-China.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

2011

 The [Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Danyang-Kunshan-Grand-Bridge) opened, becoming the longest bridge in the world. Extending 102.4 miles (164.8 km), it connects [Nanjing](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nanjing-China) and [Shanghai](https://www.britannica.com/place/Shanghai), the largest city in China.

1986

 In [_Bowers_ v. _Hardwick_](https://www.britannica.com/event/Bowers-v-Hardwick), the [U.S. Supreme Court](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Supreme-Court-of-the-United-States) ruled that states could criminalize gay sex between consenting adults; the decision was overturned by [_Lawrence_ v. _Texas_](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Lawrence-v-Texas) in 2003. _[[Why are there nine justices on the U.S. Supreme Court?](https://www.britannica.com/story/why-are-there-nine-justices-on-the-us-supreme-court)]_

 Swimmer [Michael Phelps](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Michael-Phelps), the most-decorated Olympic athlete with 28 medals, was born in Baltimore, Maryland. _[[Who is the second-most-decorated Olympic athlete?](https://www.britannica.com/topic/list-of-athletes-with-the-most-Olympic-medals)]_

1966

 The [National Organization for Women](https://www.britannica.com/topic/National-Organization-for-Women) (NOW) was founded with the mission of promoting [equal rights](https://www.britannica.com/topic/gender-equality) for women; [Betty Friedan](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Betty-Friedan) served as its first president.

1936

 American author [Margaret Mitchell](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Margaret-Mitchell) published [Gone with the Wind](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Gone-with-the-Wind-novel), a sweeping romance set during the [Civil War](https://www.britannica.com/event/American-Civil-War); the novel later won a [Pulitzer Prize](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Pulitzer-Prize) and was adapted into a [hugely successful film](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Gone-with-the-Wind-film-by-Fleming). _[[Take our quiz about famous authors](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/famous-authors).]_

 An [enormous aerial explosion](https://www.britannica.com/event/Tunguska-event) flattened approximately 2,000 square km (500,000 acres) of pine forest near the Podkamennaya Tunguska River in central [Siberia](https://www.britannica.com/place/Siberia). _[[Read more about this and six other strange disasters](https://www.britannica.com/list/7-strange-disasters).]_
