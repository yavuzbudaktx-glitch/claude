 1946: The atomic origins of the bikini

 On this day in 1946, a French designer unveiled a bombastic (or bombtastic?) new swimsuit and named it the bikini, after[Bikini atoll](https://www.britannica.com/place/Bikini-atoll-Marshall-Islands)in the [Marshall Islands](https://www.britannica.com/place/Marshall-Islands), the site of recent U.S. nuclear testing. While the bathing suit was considered scandalous at the time—it was just 30 square inches of fabric—it has since become an exploration of one’s agency, expression, and relationship with one’s body. As fashion icon[Diana Vreeland](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Diana-Vreeland)said, “The bikini is the most important thing since the atom bomb.”

Twenty-sixth Amendment certified

 Certified this day in 1971, the[Twenty-sixth Amendment](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Twenty-sixth-Amendment)to the[U.S. Constitution](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Constitution-of-the-United-States-of-America)lowered the age of eligibility to [vote](https://www.britannica.com/topic/suffrage) to 18. _[[Find out what the voting age is in every country in the world](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Voting-Ages-Around-the-World).]_

P.T. Barnum

[P.T. Barnum](https://www.britannica.com/biography/P-T-Barnum), born this day in 1810, was an American showman who built his fame on outrageous stunts, the exploitation of “[human curiosities](https://www.britannica.com/art/freak-show),” and brazen deceit. He also, with [James A. Bailey](https://www.britannica.com/biography/James-A-Bailey), made the American[circus](https://www.britannica.com/art/circus-theatrical-entertainment)a popular and gigantic spectacle.

1946

 The first bikini, designed by French engineer Louis Réard, made its debut in Paris; the [swimsuit](https://www.britannica.com/topic/swimsuit) was named for the [Bikini](https://www.britannica.com/place/Bikini-atoll-Marshall-Islands) atoll, which, just days earlier, had been the site of the world’s first peacetime atomic-weapons test. _[[Can you name these historical fashion trends?](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/quiz-name-these-historical-fashion-trends)]_
