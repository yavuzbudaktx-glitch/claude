 1963: The ZIP code’s rocky introduction

 On this day in 1963, the[U.S. Post Office Department](https://www.britannica.com/topic/United-States-Postal-Service)debuted the[ZIP code](https://www.britannica.com/topic/ZIP-Code), and it ushered in the age of electronic mail sorting. Americans were reluctant at first—they’d barely gotten used to telephone area codes. It took almost three years for enough people to embrace the five-digit code for electronic scanning to be widely deployed. But once those mechanized optical scanners got going, they could fly, sorting 36,000 pieces of mail each hour. In 1983 the USPS added the ZIP+4.

Dominion of Canada established

 The [Dominion](https://www.britannica.com/topic/dominion-British-Commonwealth) of [Canada](https://www.britannica.com/place/Canada) was formed this day in 1867, an event subsequently celebrated as an annual Canadian holiday (its current name, [Canada Day](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Canada-Day), was adopted in 1982) marked by parades, fireworks, and the display of flags. _[[Sort fact from fiction in our quiz about Canada](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/exploring-canada-fact-or-fiction).]_
