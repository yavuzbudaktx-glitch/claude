![Image 6: The Uruguay national soccer team poses together on the field before the 1930 World Cup final, with eleven players in uniform, some kneeling and some standing.](https://cdn.britannica.com/07/285107-050-84D8469B/The-Victorious-Uruguay-National-Soccer-Team-During-The-1930-World-Cup-Final.jpg?w=356&h=200&c=crop)

 © Hulton Pictures—Allsport/Getty Images

[The first World Cup kicks off](https://www.britannica.com/sports/World-Cup-football)

 On this day in 1930, 13 teams across Europe and the Americas took the field in [Montevideo](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montevideo), [Uruguay](https://www.britannica.com/place/Uruguay), for the start of the first [FIFA World Cup](https://www.britannica.com/sports/World-Cup-football). The two opening matches kicked off simultaneously, and French forward Lucien Laurent scored the first goal in World Cup history—reportedly in falling snow. Uruguay would go on to defeat Argentina to become the first World Cup champions. _[[Explore some FIFA World Cup records](https://www.britannica.com/sports/FIFA-World-Cup-Records)]_

 Chinese writer and [human rights](https://www.britannica.com/topic/human-rights) activist [Liu Xiaobo](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Liu-Xiaobo), who advocated for democratic reforms and was the first Chinese citizen to be awarded (2010) the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Winners-of-the-Nobel-Prize-for-Peace-1856940), died at age 61. _[[Take our quiz about the Nobel Prize](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/nobel-prize).]_

2016

[Theresa May](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Theresa-May), the [Conservative Party](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Conservative-Party-political-party-United-Kingdom) leader, became the second woman prime minister of the [United Kingdom](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Kingdom), replacing [David Cameron](https://www.britannica.com/biography/David-Cameron), who resigned after the country [voted](https://www.britannica.com/) to leave the [European Union](https://www.britannica.com/topic/European-Union). _[[Test your knowledge of British culture and politics](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/british-culture-and-politics).]_

![Image 12: Honeybee host](https://cdn.britannica.com/93/272593-050-5D6D6DAF/A-Varroa-mite-sits-on-a-honeybee-Germany-2019.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

 ©Sebastian Gollnow—picture alliance/Getty Images

2007

 The U.S. Department of Agriculture announced plans to address [colony collapse disorder](https://www.britannica.com/science/colony-collapse-disorder), which had been first reported several months earlier. A disorder affecting [honeybees](https://www.britannica.com/animal/honeybee), it represents a serious threat to American agriculture. _[[Learn why bees, along with wasps and ants, are one of the most ecologically important animal groups on Earth](https://www.britannica.com/explore/hymenoptera-ants-bees-wasps).]_

![Image 13: Live Aid, 1985](https://cdn.britannica.com/38/200938-050-E22981D1/Freddie-Mercury-Live-Aid-Queen-Wembley-Stadium-July-13-1985.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

1985

 The benefit concert [Live Aid](https://www.britannica.com/event/Live-Aid) was held simultaneously at Wembley Stadium in [London](https://www.britannica.com/place/London) and JFK Stadium in [Philadelphia](https://www.britannica.com/place/Philadelphia). Organized by [Bob Geldof](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Bob-Geldof) and Midge Ure, the event drew an estimated 1.5 billion television viewers and raised millions of dollars for famine relief in [Ethiopia](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ethiopia). _[[Take our music quiz](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/music-quiz).]_

 Backed by trumpeter [Harry James](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Harry-James), American singer [Frank Sinatra](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-Sinatra) recorded his first single, From the Bottom of My Heart. _[[How much do you know about Frank Sinatra?](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/frank-sinatra-quiz)]_

![Image 16: "The Death of Marat" by Jacques-Louis David shows the slain Jean-Paul Marat slumped in a bath, holding a letter, with a wooden box inscribed "A Marat David" in the foreground.](https://cdn.britannica.com/21/7921-050-2742DCFE/Death-of-Marat-Jacques-Louis-David-Royal-Museums-1793.jpg?w=260&h=196&c=crop)

1793

[Jean-Paul Marat](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Jean-Paul-Marat), a leader of the radical [Montagnard](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Montagnard-French-history) faction during the [French Revolution](https://www.britannica.com/event/French-Revolution), was stabbed to death in his bath by [Charlotte Corday](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Charlotte-Corday), a young [Girondin](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Girondin) supporter who was subsequently guillotined. _[[How much do you know about French history?](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/france-a-history-quiz)]_
