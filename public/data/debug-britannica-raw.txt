 1928: The commercial introduction of sliced bread

 On this day in 1928, [bread](https://www.britannica.com/topic/bread) loaves that had been presliced—with the first commercial bread slicer—were sold for the very first time. Advertised as “The Greatest Forward Step in the Baking Industry Since Bread Was Wrapped” and “a fine loaf sold a better way,” the loaves were produced by Chillicothe Baking Company in [Chillicothe](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chillicothe-Missouri), Missouri and cut using a slicing machine designed by jeweler Otto Rohwedder.

Hawaiian Islands annexed by the United States

 The [U.S. Congress](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Congress-of-the-United-States) annexed [Hawaii](https://www.britannica.com/place/Hawaii-state) through a joint resolution signed by President [William McKinley](https://www.britannica.com/biography/William-McKinley) on this day in 1898, paving the way for the islands to become a territory (1900) and later a U.S. state (1959). _[[Sort fact from fiction in our quiz about Hawaii](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/hawaii-fact-or-fiction).]_

Marc Chagall

Born this day in 1887, French artist [Marc Chagall](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Marc-Chagall) created images based on emotional and poetic associations, and his early works, which predated [Surrealism](https://www.britannica.com/art/Surrealism), were among the first expressions of psychic reality in modern art.
