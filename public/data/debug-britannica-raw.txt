 1918: The end of House Romanov

 On this day in 1918 Tsar [Nicholas II](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nicholas-II-tsar-of-Russia), his wife, [Alexandra](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Alexandra-empress-consort-of-Russia), their five children, and four loyal servants were executed by [Bolshevik](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Bolshevik) forces in [Yekaterinburg](https://www.britannica.com/place/Yekaterinburg). The killings ended more than 300 years of [Romanov](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Romanov-dynasty) rule and became one of the defining episodes of the [Russian Revolution](https://www.britannica.com/event/Russian-Revolution), fueling decades of rumors that one or more of the royal children—especially [Anastasia](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Anastasia-Russian-grand-duchess)—had survived.
