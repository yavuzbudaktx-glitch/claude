 1861: Wild Bill Hickok’s first gunfight

 On this day in 1861, in a postal station in Rock Creek, [Nebraska](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nebraska-state), a legend was born. The man who would become known as [Wild Bill Hickok](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Wild-Bill-Hickok) is said to have fought his first gunfight there. Then known as “Duck Bill” (a derogatory reference to Hickok’s thin lips), Hickok confronted a group of men led by local bully and landowner David McCanles over the station’s rent, leaving McCanles and two of his men dead. But what really happened? Read on to discover how the legend was made.

 The [Medal of Honor](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Medal-of-Honor-United-States-military-decoration), awarded for battlefield bravery, was created for the [U.S. Army](https://www.britannica.com/topic/The-United-States-Army). _[[Take our military history buff quiz](https://www.britannica.com/quiz/military-history-buff-quiz).]_

1776

 British explorer Capt.[James Cook](https://www.britannica.com/biography/James-Cook)set out on his third and final voyage to find the Northwest Passage through northern [Canada](https://www.britannica.com/place/Canada) and [Alaska](https://www.britannica.com/place/Alaska) or the Northeast Passage around [Siberia](https://www.britannica.com/place/Siberia), linking the Atlantic and Pacific oceans. Sailing on the _Resolution_, Cook was unsuccessful, and the voyage led to his death. In a brief confrontation with Native Hawaiians over the theft of a cutter, Cook was killed on the beach at Kealakekua Bay in February 1779.
