 1969: First contact with a hazardous surface

 On this day in 1969, the[Apollo 11](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Apollo-11)mission put men on the[Moon](https://www.britannica.com/place/Moon) for the first time. As[Neil Armstrong](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Neil-Armstrong)’s boots made contact with the lunar surface, his words became etched in history: “That’s one small step for [a] man, one giant leap for mankind.” But what was he actually stepping on? While the Moon’s surface is powdery, lunar dust is incredibly sharp—similar to countless microscopic knives. This dust plagued all future lunar expeditions.

![Image 8: Hitler and Mussolini after the July Plot failed](https://cdn.britannica.com/21/158221-050-CD833C16/Adolf-Hitler-field-headquarters-Benito-Mussolini-Rastenburg-July-1944.jpg?w=356&h=200&c=crop)

 Keystone—Hulton Archive/Getty Images

[Operation Valkyrie failed](https://www.britannica.com/event/July-Plot)

 On this day in 1944, German military leaders attempted to assassinate [Adolf Hitler](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Adolf-Hitler). The plan, often called the [July Plot](https://www.britannica.com/event/July-Plot), failed. A bomb in a suitcase was detonated during Hitler’s meeting with military leaders, but he suffered only minor injuries.
