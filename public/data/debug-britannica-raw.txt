 1807: Napoleon Bonaparte signs the second Treaty of Tilsit

 French Emperor [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Napoleon-I) signed the second [Treaty of Tilsit](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Treaties-of-Tilsit) with Russia and Prussia following victories at [Jena](https://www.britannica.com/event/Battle-of-Jena) and [Friedland](https://www.britannica.com/event/Battle-of-Friedland-1807). France and Russia were now allies, and Prussia was largely left to its fate. It was yet another episode in the [Napoleonic Wars](https://www.britannica.com/event/Napoleonic-Wars) and the emperor’s attempts to dominate Europe. Perhaps more interesting than the treaty? The rabbit hunt that followed. Read on for that story in which rabbits sent Napoleon running.

O.J. Simpson

Born this day in 1947, [O.J. Simpson](https://www.britannica.com/biography/O-J-Simpson) was an outstanding [football](https://www.britannica.com/sports/American-football) running back, winning the 1968 [Heisman Trophy](https://www.britannica.com/sports/Heisman-Trophy) and setting an NFL single-season record for rushing yards. By the end of his life, he was better known for a [1995 trial](https://www.britannica.com/event/O-J-Simpson-trial) in which he was acquitted of murder charges.
