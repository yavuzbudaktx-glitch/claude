 1863: The end of the Battle of Gettysburg

 On this day in 1863, the [Battle of Gettysburg](https://www.britannica.com/event/Battle-of-Gettysburg) ended with a crushing Southern defeat and marked the turning point in the [American Civil War](https://www.britannica.com/event/American-Civil-War). The fighting began two days earlier when Gen. [Robert E. Lee](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Robert-E-Lee) led Confederate troops into Pennsylvania and attacked Union forces. The climax occurred on July 3, with the spectacular failure of Pickett’s Charge, in which Confederate troops tried to break through Union lines. Lee was forced to retreat the next day. Gettysburg was the bloodiest battle of the war, with more than 50,000 casualties.

[Quebec founded](https://www.britannica.com/place/Quebec)

 On this day in 1608, [Samuel de Champlain](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Samuel-de-Champlain) founded the city of [Quebec](https://www.britannica.com/place/Quebec), the first permanent European base in [Canada](https://www.britannica.com/place/Canada). Its splendid views and unique character were noted as early as 1842 during a visit by[Charles Dickens](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Charles-Dickens-British-novelist), who called Quebec the “Gibraltar of North America.”
